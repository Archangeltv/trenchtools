trench.tools is designed to earn trust structurally rather than ask for it. This page states plainly what the app is and is not able to do, and how to verify its behavior.

## What the app can and cannot do

**It can:**

- Read public chain data over the RPC.
- Build transactions and hand them to your wallet for approval.
- Compute things locally: hashes, conversions, merkle trees, vanity keys, and signature recovery.

**It cannot:**

- Move funds without a signature. Every transaction is signed in your wallet for a specific action; there is no custody and no admin key.
- See your private key or seed phrase. It never asks for them and never handles them.
- Store or transmit your data to a backend. There is no backend or database. See [Privacy by design](/docs/concepts/privacy-by-design).
- Auto-sign or bundle hidden actions. Each transaction is a distinct prompt you review.

The residual trust is ordinary and unavoidable: you trust that the frontend you loaded builds the transaction it says it does. The mitigations for that are client-side verification and wallet inspection before signing, below.

<Callout type="warn">
  On-chain actions are irreversible. The app's safety guarantees are about
  custody and privacy; they do not protect you from approving a transaction
  with the wrong address or amount. Always review each transaction in your
  wallet before signing.
</Callout>

## Verifying app behavior

Because trench.tools runs entirely in your browser without backend servers or databases, you can inspect everything it does in real time:

- **Watch the network.** Open your browser's developer tools and inspect the Network tab while using any tool. You should see requests only to the chain RPC, the Blockscout explorer API, and (only in the decoder, for unknown selectors) openchain.xyz. The offline tools make no network requests at all.
- **Inspect each transaction in your wallet.** Your wallet shows the exact contract call, target address, parameters, and ETH value before you sign. That is the final, authoritative check on what any tool is asking you to do.
- **Independent key handling.** Offline utilities such as the Vanity Address Generator and Unit Converter generate keys and hashes locally in your browser memory and never broadcast them.
